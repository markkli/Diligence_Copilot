# Diligence Report: Guidewire

## Commercial Diligence Memo
[Executive Summary]
- Company description
  - Provider of core P&C insurance platform combining core systems of record with digital, analytics, and AI; flagship products InsuranceSuite and InsuranceNow delivered primarily as cloud-based subscription services on Guidewire Cloud Platform (GWCP) (10-K.pdf, p.7; 10-K.pdf, p.12).
  - Platform includes PolicyCenter, ClaimCenter, BillingCenter; Marketplace with partner-developed integrations; GWCP positioned as insurers’ operational/financial backbone (10-K.pdf, p.12; 10-K.pdf, p.15; IR.pdf, p.6).
  - Scale indicators: 349 core customers, $775B Direct Written Premium (DWP) under contract, runs in 43 countries, 9 of top 10 global insurers use Guidewire (IR.pdf, p.6).
- Recommendation (Invest / Pass / Further Diligence)
  - Further Diligence
- Top 3 reasons
  1. Market-leading, vertically focused platform with deep embedment in insurer operations and high customer penetration (IR.pdf, p.6; 10-K.pdf, p.12).
  2. Recurring cloud subscription pricing tied to Direct Written Premium and long contract durations (typically five years, sometimes seven+) provides revenue visibility (10-K.pdf, p.7).
  3. Material execution and market risks (customer concentration, lengthy sales/implementation cycles, competitive fragmentation, cloud-cost/regulatory exposure) require deeper financial and operational validation (IR.pdf, p.2; 10-K.pdf, p.7; 10-K.pdf, p.21).

## Company Overview
[Company Description]
- What the company does: Provides a cloud-based platform and core systems of record for property & casualty (P&C) insurers to support policy administration, claims management, billing, digital engagement, analytics, and AI capabilities (10-K.pdf, Pages 7, 11).
- Products / services: Core products are Guidewire InsuranceSuite (PolicyCenter, ClaimCenter, BillingCenter) and Guidewire InsuranceNow; complemented by Guidewire Cloud Platform (GWCP), digital engagement, analytics, Guidewire Marketplace, and rating management (10-K.pdf, Pages 11, 12; IR.pdf, Page 6).
- Position in the value chain: Serves as insurers’ operational backbone and system-of-record provider for the P&C lifecycle, embedding cloud platform services, analytics, digital engagement, and a partner marketplace to enable insurer operations and innovation (10-K.pdf, Pages 7, 11; IR.pdf, Page 6).

[Business Model]
- Revenue model: Primarily cloud-based subscription services for core products; some cloud-delivered products priced based on usage or other metrics (10-K.pdf, Page 7).
- Customer base: Serves insurers of all sizes (global carriers to regional/local providers); under contract for at least one core module: 349 core customers; Direct Written Premium (DWP) under contract $775B; 9 of the top 10 global insurers use Guidewire (IR.pdf, Page 6; 10-K.pdf, Page 7).
- Pricing / monetization approach: Subscription pricing generally based on amount of DWP managed on the platform; certain cloud products priced by usage or other metrics; initial subscription agreements generally five years (sometimes seven+ years); subscription revenue recognized ratably over the contract term (10-K.pdf, Page 7).

[Operating Footprint]
- Revenue segments: Not disclosed.
- Geography: Platform runs in 43 countries; InsuranceNow currently only available in the United States; products are localized for regulatory, language, and currency requirements to support insurers worldwide (IR.pdf, Page 6; 10-K.pdf, Pages 7, 11).
- End markets: Property & casualty (P&C) insurance industry, including global carriers, regional/local insurers, mid-market carriers and managing general agents (10-K.pdf, Pages 7, 11; IR.pdf, Page 6).

## Investment Committee Summary
[Investment Thesis]
- Core thesis: Guidewire is a core P&C insurance platform provider whose cloud-first suite (InsuranceSuite, InsuranceNow) and Guidewire Cloud Platform (GWCP) drive multi-year, subscription-based relationships with insurers, supported by an ecosystem of SIs and a Marketplace (10-K pp.7, 31; 10-K p.14).
- Why now: Industry shift to cloud, digital engagement, analytics and AI increases demand for cloud-native core systems; Guidewire reports most new sales are for InsuranceSuite and is focused on cloud subscriptions and cloud operations (10-Q p.31; 10-K p.7; 10-K p.21).

[Upside Case]
- Cloud migration accelerates subscription ARR as more customers move from on‑prem to GWCP, increasing predictable revenue (10-Q p.31; 10-K p.7).
- New/expanded product capabilities (recently announced PricingCenter and UnderwritingCenter) and embedded analytics/AI could drive upsell across core applications (10-Q p.31).
- Guidewire Marketplace and strong SI relationships increase distribution, validation, and faster adoption of complementary solutions (10-K p.7; 10-K p.21).
- Localization investments enable international expansion where market requirements and regulation create high switching costs (10-K p.21; 10-Q p.31).

[Downside Case]
- Long, complex sales and implementation cycles and reliance on a relatively small number of large customers create concentration and negotiating leverage risk (IR.pdf p.2; 10-Q p.31).
- Highly competitive and fragmented P&C software market could pressure pricing and require additional investment to maintain differentiation (10-K p.21).
- Dependence on quality of professional services and third‑party SI partners; service revenue has lower gross margins than license/subscription/support (IR.pdf p.2; 10-Q p.31).
- Cloud operations, data security, and reliance on third‑party providers present operational and regulatory risks (IR.pdf p.2; 10-K p.21).
- Evolving AI/ML regulatory and development risks could materially affect product use and go‑to‑market (IR.pdf p.2).

[Key Assumptions]
- Continued industry shift to cloud-based core insurance systems and willingness of insurers to sign multi-year subscription contracts (10-Q p.31; 10-K p.7).
- Guidewire can keep cloud customers on current releases and deliver frequent product updates (10-Q p.31).
- SI and professional services ecosystem remains effective in implementations and does not materially degrade customer outcomes (10-K p.7; IR.pdf p.2).
- Investments in localization, cloud operations, analytics and security are sufficient to compete internationally and comply with local regulations (10-K p.21; 10-Q p.31).
- No major cloud outages, data breaches, or third‑party failures that would materially harm reputation or renewals (IR.pdf p.2).

[Open Diligence Questions]

- Revenue concentration: % of revenue from top N customers and customer retention rates. Not disclosed.
- Financial impact of migration from on‑prem to cloud on gross margins and operating margins (service vs subscription mix). Not disclosed (IR.pdf p.2 notes services have lower margins but no quantification).
- Adoption timelines, contract terms, and revenue contribution for PricingCenter and UnderwritingCenter. Not disclosed.
- GWCP operational metrics: uptime, incident history, and third‑party provider dependencies. Not disclosed.
- Typical and median sales and implementation cycle lengths by product and customer segment. Not disclosed.
- International revenue split and pace of localization-led wins by geography. Not disclosed.
- Specific competitive positioning versus emerging insurtechs and consolidation activity in P&C software market. Not disclosed (10-K p.21 describes market as competitive/fragmented but lacks competitor details).

[Preliminary Recommendation]
- Further Diligence
- Rationale: Product set, multi-year subscription model, and ecosystem suggest durable, sticky revenue potential (10-Q p.31; 10-K p.7), but material gaps on ARR, customer concentration, margin impact of cloud transition, GWCP operational metrics, and adoption of new modules require validation before an invest/pass decision (IR.pdf p.2; 10-K p.21).

## Diligence Questions
1)
- Question: What is the current revenue and renewal concentration by customer (top 10 and top 3), and how sensitive projected ARR and cash flow are to non-renewal or downsizing by these customers?
- Why it matters: Guidewire flags reliance on a relatively small number of large customers and long contract terms; loss or renegotiation by a few customers could materially change growth and cash forecasts.
- Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p2; 10-K.pdf p7

2)
- Question: How have discounts and fixed-fee arrangements to encourage cloud migrations impacted services margin and unit economics over the last three fiscal years, and what assumptions underlie margin recovery in management projections?
- Why it matters: Management warns services margin erosion from discounts and fixed-fee deals; sustained erosion would depress gross and operating margins and alter valuation.
- Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p44; 10-Q.pdf p74

3)
- Question: What governance, SLAs, and capacity metrics are in place for global SI partners and subcontractors, and what percentage of recent cloud migrations required Guidewire to add internal resources or accept margin-reducing subcontracting?
- Why it matters: Implementation quality of SIs and subcontracting practices directly affect migration success, customer satisfaction, time-to-value, and services profitability.
- Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p44; 10-Q.pdf p74

4)
- Question: How is DWP-based pricing stress-tested against shifts in customers’ underwriting mix, reinsurance arrangements, or regulatory DWP definitions, and what downside scenarios have been modeled for DWP contraction?
- Why it matters: Core pricing and ARR are tied to DWP under management ($775B under contract); changes in how customers report/manage DWP would directly affect revenue.
- Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p6; 10-K.pdf p7; IR.pdf p11

5)
- Question: What are the typical timelines, resource requirements, and key failure modes observed in migrations from self‑managed InsuranceSuite to GWCP, and how do these compare to management’s go‑forward migration capacity assumptions?
- Why it matters: Sales cycles and migrations are lengthy/complex; underestimated timelines or underestimated resource needs create backlog, higher services costs, and slower subscription ramp.
- Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p2; 10-K.pdf p7

6)
- Question: Which specific product areas or customer segments show signs of weakening market acceptance, and what KPIs (churn, renewal rates, feature adoption) does management monitor to detect product obsolescence versus competitive displacement?
- Why it matters: The 10-K warns that failure of established products to meet demand would harm growth; early detection metrics are critical to avoid irreversible revenue loss.
- Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p44

7)
- Question: What are the regulatory, model‑risk, and commercialization controls around Guidewire’s GenAI Services (LLM/RAG/Prompt Management), and how have potential regulatory constraints been incorporated into revenue and product road‑map assumptions?
- Why it matters: Guidewire is promoting GenAI capabilities but also notes AI development and evolving regulation as material risks; regulatory limits could impair product adoption and create liability.
- Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p2; IR.pdf p6; IR.pdf p11

8)
- Question: How granular and up-to-date is Guidewire’s local content and compliance roadmap for each priority geography, and what are the estimated incremental costs and timelines to enter/scale in markets where localization is incomplete?
- Why it matters: Growth depends on efficient international expansion and local content; gaps can slow sales, extend migrations, and raise service costs.
- Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p7; IR.pdf p6

9)
- Question: What is the inventory of third‑party cloud or infrastructure providers and the contingency plans/SLA breaches recorded to date; how would a significant third‑party outage or security breach affect customer SLAs, liabilities, and retention?
- Why it matters: Management flags data security breaches and third‑party service failures as risks; such events could trigger churn, indemnities, and reputational damage.
- Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p2; 10-K.pdf p44

10)
- Question: How does management quantify the competitive threat from technology substitutes (new vendors or insurtechs) and technological advances that might reduce the appeal of Guidewire’s platform, and what R&D/capex is committed to defend against these scenarios?
- Why it matters: The 10-K states competitors’ advances and tech shifts could reduce product appeal; inability to maintain a technological edge would impair future revenue.
- Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p44; IR.pdf p6

11)
- Question: For recent large account wins that materially expanded DWP commitments (e.g., Aviva UK, Tokio Marine), what escalation clauses, implementation milestones, and revenue recognition contingencies exist if migrations are delayed or scope is reduced?
- Why it matters: Large announced wins drive forward guidance but may carry execution risk; delays or scope reductions would lower near-term ARR and cash flow.
- Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p11; 10-K.pdf p7

12)
- Question: What workforce planning, utilization, and hiring assumptions underlie support for accelerated cloud migrations, and what trigger metrics would prompt management to shift between hiring vs. subcontracting to avoid margin leakage?
- Why it matters: Services margin erosion can occur from pre‑hiring or underutilization; clarity on staffing strategy affects margin sensitivity and execution risk.
- Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p44; 10-Q.pdf p74
