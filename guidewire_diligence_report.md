
# Diligence Report: Guidewire

## Commercial Diligence Memo
[Executive Summary]
- Company description
  - Provider of core software platform for property & casualty (P&C) insurers: InsuranceSuite and InsuranceNow delivered primarily as cloud subscription via Guidewire Cloud Platform (GWCP); also offers digital, analytics/AI capabilities and Guidewire Marketplace (10-K.pdf, p.7; 10-K.pdf, p.81; 10-K.pdf, p.15).
- Recommendation (Invest / Pass / Further Diligence)
  - Further Diligence
- Top 3 reasons
  - Entrenched, mission‑critical platform with recurring, multi‑year subscription contracts and DWP‑based pricing; platform is central to insurer operations, creating high switching costs (10-K.pdf, p.7; 10-K.pdf, p.81).
  - Large commercial footprint and scale: 349 core customers under contract and $775B of Direct Written Premium (DWP) under contract; presence in 43 countries and adoption by 9 of the top 10 global insurers (IR.pdf, p.6).
  - Material execution and market risks disclosed (customer concentration, long sales/implementation cycles, intense/fragmented competition, cloud migration and AI/regulatory risks) that require validation against financials and conversion metrics (IR.pdf, p.2; 10-K.pdf, p.21; 10-K.pdf, p.7).

[Business Model]
- Revenue streams
  - Primarily subscription services for core products (cloud-based); subscription pricing generally based on Direct Written Premium (DWP) managed; some cloud products priced on usage or other metrics (10-K.pdf, p.7).
  - Marketplace partner integrations and partner-developed applications complement platform (10-K.pdf, p.15).
- Key segments
  - Core InsuranceSuite customers (PolicyCenter, ClaimCenter, BillingCenter) and InsuranceNow; digital engagement, analytics/AI, and Marketplace ecosystem (10-K.pdf, p.81; 10-K.pdf, p.15).
- Geography
  - Global operations: products localized for multiple countries/states; platform run in 43 countries (10-K.pdf, p.81; IR.pdf, p.6).

  - Not disclosed

  - Not disclosed
- Key drivers
  - Insurers shifting to cloud-delivered core systems and modern digital/analytics capabilities (10-K.pdf, p.7; 10-K.pdf, p.81).
  - Need for localization (regulatory, language, currency) and ongoing updates driving recurring product development (10-K.pdf, p.21; 10-K.pdf, p.81).
  - Partner ecosystem and Marketplace enabling faster innovation and differentiation (10-K.pdf, p.15).

[Competitive Positioning]
- Key competitors
  - Not disclosed (10-K.pdf, p.21 notes a highly competitive and fragmented market but does not name competitors).
- Differentiation
  - GWCP positioned as the insurer’s operational and financial backbone with integrated core apps, ongoing cloud operations, and localization—plus a vetted Marketplace and PartnerConnect ecosystem (IR.pdf, p.6; 10-K.pdf, p.15; 10-K.pdf, p.81).
- Positioning
  - Market leader in P&C core platform per company materials: large installed base, significant DWP under contract, and adoption by top global carriers (IR.pdf, p.6).

[Key Risks]
- Ranked bullet list
  1. Reliance on a relatively small number of large customers and their negotiating leverage / renewals risk (IR.pdf, p.2).
  2. Long, complex sales and implementation cycles, especially for migrations to GWCP (10-K.pdf, p.7; IR.pdf, p.2).
  3. Highly competitive and fragmented market with new entrants and consolidators (10-K.pdf, p.21).
  4. Execution risk on cloud operations, security, and potential service interruptions (10-K.pdf, p.21; 10-K.pdf, p.15).
  5. International expansion and localization complexity (regulation, language, currency) requiring ongoing investment (10-K.pdf, p.21; 10-K.pdf, p.81).
  6. Technology and regulatory uncertainty around AI/ML and related compliance (IR.pdf, p.2).

[Upside Opportunities]
- Ranked bullet list
  1. Continued migration and expansion to GWCP (entrenchment, subscription revenue growth potential) (10-K.pdf, p.7; 10-K.pdf, p.81).
  2. Marketplace and PartnerConnect ecosystem expansion (315+ Ready for Guidewire integrations; potential for faster innovation and revenue adjacencies) (10-K.pdf, p.15).
  3. Growth of analytics and AI/GenAI services embedded in platform (IR.pdf, p.6; 10-K.pdf, p.81).
  4. Cross‑sell/up‑sell across core modules (PolicyCenter/ClaimCenter/BillingCenter) and cloud services due to modular architecture (10-K.pdf, p.81).
  5. High switching costs and referenceability from large global customers support renewal/expansion potential (10-K.pdf, p.7; IR.pdf, p.6).

  - Further Diligence

  - Competitive pressure and pace of innovation from insurtechs and platform providers (10-K.pdf, p.21).

## Company Overview
[Company Description]
- What the company does — Provides a core software platform and cloud services for property & casualty (P&C) insurers to manage policy, claims, billing, pricing, digital engagement, analytics and AI-enabled capabilities (10-K.pdf, p.7).
- Products / services — InsuranceSuite (PolicyCenter, ClaimCenter, BillingCenter) delivered as a service on Guidewire Cloud Platform (GWCP) hosted on AWS; InsuranceNow (cloud-based, U.S.-only, suited to mid‑market carriers and managing general agents); complementary digital, analytics, Rating Management, AI services, and Guidewire Marketplace (10-K.pdf, pp.11–12; IR.pdf, p.6).
- Position in the value chain — Acts as the P&C industry’s core systems-of-record and cloud backbone (GWCP) embedded across underwriting, pricing, policy, claims and billing; operates a partner/marketplace ecosystem and sells via direct global sales and third‑party system integrators (10-K.pdf, pp.7,11; IR.pdf, p.6).

[Business Model]
- Revenue model — Primarily cloud-based subscription services for platform and cloud-delivered products; some cloud products priced based on usage or other metrics (10-K.pdf, p.7).
- Customer base — Serves insurers of all sizes (global, regional, local); 349 core customers under contract; Direct Written Premium under contract $775B; runs in 43 countries; 9 of the Top 10 Global Insurers use Guidewire (10-K.pdf, p.7; IR.pdf, p.6).
- Pricing / monetization approach — Core subscription pricing generally based on the amount of Direct Written Premium (DWP) managed on the platform; initial subscription agreements generally five years (some seven+ years), with annual renewals thereafter; subscription revenue recognized ratably (10-K.pdf, p.7).

[Operating Footprint]
- Revenue segments — Subscription services for core platform and cloud‑delivered products; complementary digital, analytics, AI and marketplace offerings (10-K.pdf, pp.7,11–12). More granular segment breakdown: Not disclosed.
- Geography — Guidewire runs in 43 countries; InsuranceNow is currently available only in the United States (IR.pdf, p.6; 10-K.pdf, p.11).
- End markets — Property & Casualty (P&C) insurance industry, covering underwriting, policy administration, claims management, billing, pricing and related digital/analytics needs (10-K.pdf, pp.7,11–12).

## Diligence Questions
Question: What percentage of revenue and ARR is concentrated in the company’s largest customers (top 5 / top 10), and how frequently have those large customers exercised negotiating leverage to materially change pricing or contract terms in the last 3 years?
Why it matters: High customer-concentration and bargaining power can create renewal, pricing, and cash-flow risk that materially affects valuation and downside.
Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p.2 (“reliance on sales to, and renewals from, a relatively small number of large customers and the related substantial negotiating leverage of these customers”); IR.pdf p.6 (core customers 349; DWP under contract $775B).

Question: How much revenue (and gross margin) has migrated from license/support to cloud subscription over the last 3 years, and what is the projected margin profile by year for cloud subscription vs services over the medium term?
Why it matters: Migration to cloud changes revenue mix and has lower services margins; incorrect assumptions could materially depress margins and cash conversion.
Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p.7 (shift to cloud-delivered subscription; pricing by DWP); 10-K.pdf p.44 & 10-Q.pdf p.74 (services revenue produces lower gross margins; risk of erosion of services margin).

Question: What discounts, fixed-fee commitments, or other concessions have been used to incent customers to move to GWCP, and how do those concessions affect forward services and subscription margins?
Why it matters: Discounts and fixed-fee arrangements can lock in lower margins and increase execution risk if migration / delivery costs exceed assumptions.
Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p.44 & 10-Q.pdf p.74 (erosion of services margin due to discounts encouraging cloud agreements; fixed fee arrangements).

Question: What are the primary drivers and assumptions behind the company’s stated DWP growth in recent customer wins (e.g., Aviva UK, Tokio Marine, Zurich Germany), including timing and contractually committed DWP versus contingent/opt-in expansions?
Why it matters: Reported “expanded DWP” from wins may be timing-dependent or conditional; upside could be overstated if contingent or slow to realize.
Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p.11 (Q2’FY26 wins listing migrations and “expanded DWP significantly”); IR.pdf p.6 (DWP under contract $775B).

Question: What governance, SLAs, and oversight mechanisms exist for third‑party global system integrators (SIs) and subcontractors, and what are historical implementation defect/remediation costs and time overruns attributable to SI work?
Why it matters: Implementation quality by SIs directly affects customer success, churn, cost-to-serve, and reputation — a material execution risk.
Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p.44 & 10-Q.pdf p.74 (extent and quality of implementations and migrations provided by SI partners; subcontracting services to SI partners).

Question: What are current services utilization rates, bench size, and hiring plans for professional services; and what is the sensitivity of services margin to underutilization or hiring ahead of demand?
Why it matters: Hiring/training before engagements or underutilized staff can materially erode services margins and operating profitability.
Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p.44 & 10-Q.pdf p.74 (services margin may erode if we hire and train additional services personnel prior to customer engagements or if personnel are underutilized).

Question: How is DWP measured, audited, and contractually defined in subscription pricing terms, and what controls exist to prevent customer reclassification, off-platform risk, or disputes over DWP calculation?
Why it matters: Pricing tied to DWP creates revenue sensitivity to measurement disputes or customer actions that reduce recorded DWP, impacting subscription revenue.
Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p.7 (subscription pricing for core products based on amount of Direct Written Premium managed on our platform); IR.pdf p.6 (DWP under contract $775B).

Question: What are the company’s documented availability, resiliency, and incident-history metrics (SLA performance, outages, RTO/RPO) for GWCP, and what third‑party dependencies or single points of failure exist in the cloud stack?
Why it matters: GWCP is described as insurers’ operational and financial backbone; any platform outage or third-party failure can create catastrophic customer and legal risk.
Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p.6 (GWCP is the operational and financial backbone; run as a continuously improving, secure, reliable, and scalable cloud service); IR.pdf p.2 (industry depends on precision, resilience, compliance).

Question: What regulatory, compliance, and liability controls are in place for the company’s GenAI/AI capabilities embedded in core systems, and what scenarios or jurisdictions present the highest regulatory risk?
Why it matters: Use of AI in core insurance workflows introduces evolving regulatory, compliance, and liability risk that could restrict product use or create remediation costs.
Evidence prompting the question: IR.pdf p.2 (issues in development and use of AI/ML technologies and the related evolving regulatory environment); IR.pdf p.6 (GenAI Services listed in product stack).

Question: How much of revenue and cash flow is dependent on the continued market acceptance of InsuranceSuite and InsuranceNow, and what explicit go‑to‑market and product roadmap milestones mitigate the risk of losing technological advantage to competitors?
Why it matters: Majority reliance on established products concentrates product and market-risk; failure to maintain technological edge would materially affect growth and valuation.
Evidence prompting the question: 10-K.pdf p.44 & 10-Q.pdf p.74 (derive a significant majority of revenue and cash flows from established product offerings; need to maintain market acceptance).
